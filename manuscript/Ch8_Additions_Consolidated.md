# 1 — WHY KETONES EXIST AT ALL

**Placement: where you explain that the brain can't use fatty acids.** One paragraph of precision that changes what ketosis means.

> **Worth being clear about why ketones exist.**
>
> It's your brain. Fatty acids can't get into your brain easily, so your liver converts them into ketones, which can.
>
> **Your other tissues don't need that step.** Your muscles burn fat directly. So does your heart — in fact your heart runs mostly on fat all day long, and always has.
>
> So ketones aren't how your body burns fat. **They're the workaround for the one organ that can't.** Everything else was already burning fat as it is.

# 2 — WHAT KETONES DO BESIDES FUEL YOU

**Placement: extending your clean fuel section.** This adds to what you have.

> #### Ketones Don't Just Feed Your Cells — They Instruct Them
>
> Here's something about ketones that goes beyond energy.
>
> **Beta-hydroxybutyrate acts as a signalling molecule that switches on your cells' own antioxidant defenses.**
>
> In a study published in *Science*, researchers found that BHB directly changes which genes your cells express — **turning up the specific proteins that protect against oxidative damage.** Mice given BHB in advance were dramatically protected against a poison that kills by generating exactly that kind of damage.
>
> BHB also **shuts down one of the main inflammatory switches in the body.**
>
> So ketones aren't only a fuel. **They're an instruction to clean up.** Which is the whole point of this book — the molecule isn't just energy, it's a message. And this one tells your cells to raise their defenses.
>
> It's also why ketones do things that have nothing to do with weight. The research on brain protection and inflammation is about **signalling**, not calories.

# 3 — KETOSIS IS NOT THE SAME AS FAT LOSS

**Placement: after your clean fuel section.**

This prevents the most demoralizing confusion in low-carb eating — and it now has a real mechanism behind it.

> #### A Distinction That Will Save You Months
>
> Ketosis means your body is running on fat. **It does not automatically mean it's running on *your* fat.**
>
> If you eat enough dietary fat, your body will happily burn that instead — staying in ketosis, ticking every box, and never reaching far into your own stores. **You can be genuinely fat-adapted and genuinely not losing fat.**
>
> Every marker looks right. The strips are positive. Energy is good. Cravings are gone. And nothing changes.
>
> #### And Here's the Mechanism, Because It Explains Everything
>
> Your fat cells aren't a warehouse. **They're a revolving door.**
>
> Fat is being released and repackaged constantly — right now, and while you sleep. And in fasting people, researchers have measured **as much as 60% of the fatty acids released from fat cells being put straight back into storage** rather than burned.
>
> Released. Not burned. Repackaged. Your body even **spends energy** doing it — it costs six units of cellular energy currency to reattach three fatty acids.
>
> **So being in ketosis means the door is open. It doesn't tell you which direction the traffic is going.**
>
> And what determines the direction is simple: **does your body actually need the energy?** If you've eaten enough fat to cover your requirements, there's no reason to burn what you released. So it pays the cost and puts it back.
>
> #### Which Means the Lever Is Usually Your Added Fats
>
> If you're fully adapted, feeling well, and your body composition isn't moving, look at the **added** fats — the ones not attached to a protein. Butter in coffee, extra oil, fat bombs.
>
> Those were genuinely useful during the transition. **They quietly become the reason nothing is changing.**
>
> Chapter 9 has the full picture of how this cycle works.

# 4 — YOUR CHOLESTEROL MIGHT GO UP

**Placement: after the clean fuel material.**

> #### One Thing That Surprises People
>
> A meaningful minority of people — often the leanest and most metabolically healthy, which is the odd part — see their LDL cholesterol rise substantially on a low-carbohydrate diet. Sometimes a lot.
>
> **What to do if it happens:**
>
> **Get an ApoB test.** It counts the actual number of particles in your blood rather than the cholesterol inside them, and it's the number that tells you the most.
>
> **Look at the whole picture.** Triglycerides down and HDL up alongside a higher LDL is a different situation from everything moving the wrong way.
>
> **And take it to your doctor rather than to the internet.** There are loud voices on both sides of this and neither of them knows your history. This is genuinely unresolved, and anyone telling you it's simple — in either direction — is overselling.

# 5 — THE GATE IS REAL

**Placement: where you say "fully open the fat-burning gates."**

**The best explanatory addition in the book**, and it now connects to the amino acid material.

> #### What "Opening the Gates" Actually Means
>
> I've used that phrase. Let me show you the real thing — **because there is an actual gate.**
>
> **Fat cannot burn itself.** To be used for energy, a fat molecule has to be physically carried through a specific doorway into the part of your cell that burns fuel. It can't drift in.
>
> That doorway is an enzyme called **CPT-1.**
>
> And here's what matters. **When you eat carbohydrate and insulin rises, your body produces a molecule whose entire job is building fat for storage. That molecule blocks the doorway.**
>
> So when insulin is up, **the door is shut.** Fat can be sitting right there inside the cell — plenty of it, right outside the furnace — completely unable to get in.
>
> **Not because you don't have fat to burn. Because the door is locked.**
>
> Lower insulin and the door opens. That's what carbohydrate restriction is actually doing, and it's why the *amount* of carbohydrate matters more than the calories attached to it.
>
> #### And There's a Carrier
>
> The door needs more than to be unlocked. **Something has to carry the fat through it** — a molecule called **carnitine.**
>
> Your body builds carnitine from two amino acids, **using vitamin C to assemble it.**
>
> Which is worth pausing on: **your ability to burn fat depends on getting enough protein and enough vitamin C.** The door can be wide open and still have nothing to carry fat through it.
>
> This is also why medium-chain fats — the ones concentrated in coconut oil and MCT oil — behave differently. **They don't need the door.** They get in regardless of whether insulin has locked it, which is why they help during the transition.
>
> #### Two Things That Take Two Different Amounts of Time
>
> **The door unlocking takes hours.** It happens every night while you sleep.
>
> **Building more doors — and more furnaces to feed — takes weeks.**
>
> That's the difference between being **allowed** to burn fat and being **good** at it. And it's why week six feels nothing like week one.
>
> #### And One More Thing
>
> **Muscle contraction unlocks the same door, from the other side.**
>
> Moving your body hits the exact same lock that insulin controls. Not indirectly — the same lock.
>
> That's why a ten-minute walk after dinner does more than it looks like it should. Chapter 17.

# 6 — HOW TO ACTUALLY MEASURE

**Placement: after your keto flu and transition material.**

> #### Testing, and Why Your Strips Stop Working
>
> **Urine strips** are cheap and useful for about the first two weeks. After that they become misleading.
>
> Here's why, and it catches people out constantly: **as you adapt, your body stops wasting ketones in your urine.** It's getting better at using them. So the strips fade **exactly when you're doing well** — and a lot of people quit at that point thinking they fell out of ketosis.
>
> **A blood meter** is the accurate option if you like data.
>
> **Or measure nothing.** Once you're adapted, how you feel is a better guide than a number: stable energy, cravings gone, comfortable going five or six hours without food. **If you have those, you have what you came for.**
>
> #### And Ketone Supplements?
>
> You can buy ketones in a bottle. They do raise your blood ketone level.
>
> **What they don't do is make you burn your own fat.** If anything they do the opposite in the moment — when ketones arrive from outside, your liver makes fewer of its own, because the signal to produce them has been satisfied. **You're in ketosis without being in fat-burning.**
>
> They may help during the transition. **They are not a shortcut, and a positive reading from a supplement tells you nothing about fat loss.**

# 7 — THE MOST COMMON WAY THIS GOES WRONG

**Placement: inside or immediately after "Why Keto Can Fail the Chronically Ill."**

Your section covers people who arrive depleted. **This is the failure that happens to healthy women**, and it's the most common of all.

> #### The Trap Nobody Warns You About
>
> The section above is about people who are already depleted. But there's a way this goes wrong in perfectly healthy women, and I see it more than anything else.
>
> **Low-carb eating suppresses appetite. That's one of its best features — and it's the trap.**
>
> When you're genuinely not hungry, you don't eat. Not restricting. Not white-knuckling. Just... not hungry, so lunch gets skipped, and the day ends with two meals that didn't have much in them.
>
> Do that for a few weeks and your body concludes food is scarce. **Thyroid output drops. Your cycle changes. Your hands get cold. Hair starts thinning. Progress stalls** — and then stalls harder, because the instinct is to cut further.
>
> **There's also a sex difference worth knowing.** In one study of a single day of fasting, the women's stress hormone rhythm shifted measurably — running higher and peaking earlier. **In the men, nothing moved at all.**
>
> One study, and it needs replicating. But it points the same way as everything else in this chapter: **women appear more sensitive to the stress of restriction, and most low-carb and fasting protocols were built on research in men.**
>
> #### What to Do
>
> **For your first month, don't trust your appetite as a guide to how much to eat.** Eat your protein on a schedule whether you want it or not.
>
> **And if you feel worse rather than better after the first couple of weeks — eat more.**
>
> That is almost always the correction, and it is almost always the last thing anyone tries.

# 8 — WHO NEEDS A DOCTOR INVOLVED

**Placement: expanding your chronic illness section into a clear list.**

Most of this is effectiveness. **The first item is genuine safety.**

> #### Who Should Do This With Medical Supervision
>
> #### One Important Warning About Diabetes Medication
>
> If you take medication for diabetes, **talk to your doctor before you start this.** Not because low-carb eating is wrong for you — it may be exactly right. But some medications need adjusting, and one needs real care.
>
> **There's a class of diabetes drug you need to know about.** The generic names all end in **"-flozin"** — Jardiance, Farxiga, Invokana, and others. **Check your bottle.**
>
> *These are now also prescribed for heart failure and kidney disease, sometimes in people who don't have diabetes at all. So check regardless.*
>
> These drugs work by making your kidneys flush extra sugar out through your urine. That's how they lower blood sugar.
>
> **Here's the problem when you combine them with very low carbohydrate.**
>
> Ketones are safe at the levels this chapter is about. If they climb much higher, they can turn your blood acidic — a real emergency called ketoacidosis.
>
> Normally there's a warning sign: your blood sugar goes high at the same time, and that's the flag that tells everyone something is wrong.
>
> **These medications remove the warning sign.** They keep flushing sugar out, so your blood sugar stays normal — or even low — while your ketones keep climbing.
>
> **So you can be in real trouble with a blood sugar reading that looks completely fine.** Doctors miss it for exactly this reason, and so do patients.
>
> **What to do:** don't start cutting carbohydrates until you've talked to whoever prescribes it. It's a five-minute conversation. Say: *"I'm planning to significantly reduce my carbohydrates. I know that can raise ketoacidosis risk on this medication. How do you want to handle it?"*
>
> **And know the warning signs regardless** — nausea, vomiting, stomach pain, deep or rapid breathing, unusual fatigue or confusion, fruity-smelling breath. **If those show up, get seen the same day and tell them you're on this medication and you've changed your diet. Don't wait for your blood sugar to go high, because it may not.**
>
> #### And Four More
>
> **Insulin, or the older diabetes pills** (glipizide, glyburide, glimepiride). These lower your blood sugar regardless of what you eat. Cut carbohydrates without adjusting the dose and it can drop too far. **Your prescriber needs to know first.**
>
> **Blood pressure medication.** Eating this way lowers blood pressure — that's good, and it means your dose may become too strong. If you get lightheaded standing up, **that's usually not a reason to abandon the diet. It's a reason to get your dose reviewed.**
>
> **If you're pregnant or trying to conceive**, this isn't the time.
>
> **If you've had your gallbladder removed** or don't tolerate fat well, a very high-fat diet may be genuinely difficult. **Don't conclude low-carb isn't for you before addressing digestion** — Chapter 11 covers what helps.
>
> **And if food is a source of real fear for you, or you have a history of an eating disorder**, please talk to someone who knows you before adopting any restrictive framework. This book is written for women whose relationship with food is broadly intact. If that isn't where you are, this isn't the right tool — and that's not a failing.
>
> **None of this is a warning against low-carb eating.** It's a warning against doing it without telling your doctor you've changed something significant. **She can't adjust for what she doesn't know about.**

# 9 — THE MENOPAUSE SECTION, SOURCED

**Placement: replacing your existing menopause section.**

Your claim is the **stated implication of published research in *Nature Reviews Endocrinology*** — not a keto talking point. Here it is with the armature.

> #### The Menopause Connection: Why This Matters More After 45
>
> If you're in perimenopause or past it, this may be the most important chapter in the book.
>
> **Estradiol is your brain's fuel manager.** That isn't a metaphor. Estrogen controls how your brain takes up and uses glucose — it regulates the transporters that bring sugar into your neurons and the machinery that burns it.
>
> **As estradiol declines, that system comes apart.** Research from the University of Arizona, published in *Nature Reviews Endocrinology*, describes it directly: during the perimenopausal transition, the estrogen system becomes **uncoupled** from your brain's energy machinery. The glucose transporters in your neurons decline. And brain hypometabolism — **your brain literally producing less energy** — appears alongside it.
>
> Brain imaging in postmenopausal women confirms it. There's a measurable decline in how much glucose the brain uses, **most pronounced in the same region affected earliest in Alzheimer's disease.**
>
> **This is why the brain fog is real.** It isn't stress, or age, or your imagination. **Your brain's primary fuel system is being dismantled, and nobody told you that was part of this.**
>
> #### And Here's What Your Brain Does About It
>
> It doesn't give up. **It switches fuels. It starts using ketones.**
>
> In the research, the aging female brain increases the transporters that bring ketones in and the enzymes needed to use them. **The shift happens automatically** — it's the brain's own adaptation to losing access to glucose.
>
> **So your brain is already reaching for ketones. The only question is where it gets them.**
>
> And this is the part that changes everything. If you're not producing ketones from your diet, your brain will make them from whatever it can find — **including breaking down its own white matter.** The insulation around your own neurons becomes fuel. Animal research suggests prolonged reliance on that source damages tissue.
>
> **Your brain, short of fuel, will consume itself to keep running.**
>
> When you eat in a way that produces ketones, you aren't forcing your brain onto an unnatural fuel. **You're supplying the fuel it's already asking for, so it doesn't have to take it from your own tissue.**
>
> #### The Rest of What Estradiol Was Doing
>
> Your brain isn't the only system affected. Estradiol was also:
>
> **Protecting your muscle.** Without it, muscle loss accelerates — which matters enormously, because muscle is where your blood sugar goes. Chapter 17.
>
> **Supporting your mitochondria.** Chapter 4.
>
> **Keeping inflammation down.** Chapters 10 and 11.
>
> **Influencing where you store fat** — the shift toward the middle that most women notice first, without changing anything they eat. Chapter 9.
>
> **Ketones touch every one of those.** Alternative brain fuel. Mitochondrial support. Reduced inflammatory signalling. And lower insulin, which changes where fat goes.
>
> **This is what Dr. Ben Bikman means when he says that as estradiol begins to wane, there's real justification for allowing your ketones to wax.**
>
> They aren't a replacement for estrogen. **They're support for the systems estrogen used to carry.**
>
> #### Two Honest Notes
>
> **This is not an argument against hormone therapy.** Hormone therapy addresses the decline directly, it's a legitimate medical option, and the risk picture has been substantially revised since the reporting that frightened a generation of women and their doctors. **That's a conversation for you and a practitioner who's current on the research** — and it isn't one I'm qualified to have for you. The two approaches address different parts of the same problem.
>
> **And this is active research rather than settled fact.** The brain's shift toward ketones in menopause is well documented. Whether dietary ketones improve long-term outcomes in women is still being studied — the researchers proposing it call perimenopause a **"window of opportunity,"** which is the language of a promising hypothesis, not a proven treatment. I think the reasoning is strong. **You should know it's reasoning.**

# 10 — DO YOU HAVE TO DO THIS FOREVER?

**Placement: inside or before "True Metabolic Flexibility."**

> #### Do You Have to Do This Forever?
>
> **No. And for most people, no is the better answer.**
>
> The point of the deep phase is to restore an ability you'd lost — running on fat without falling apart. **Once you have it, the goal shifts from *being in ketosis* to *being able to get there easily.***
>
> A metabolically flexible body handles a bowl of rice without a crash and slides into fat-burning overnight without drama. **That's the destination. Strict ketosis is usually the road, not the address.**
>
> How long the strict phase takes varies — a few months is common, longer if insulin resistance has been building for decades. And some people genuinely do best staying low long-term.
>
> **What matters is that you're choosing it rather than trapped in it.**
>
> If the thought of adding back a sweet potato feels frightening, that's worth paying attention to — and Chapter 18 is about exactly that.
