Breast Cancer Beyond Genetics · Part Five

Eating And Living Like Your Ancestors

Your great-grandmother managed none of this on purpose. She ate food that came from somewhere she could name, moved because life required it, went dark when the sun did, and had real gaps between meals — not because she was fasting, but because food was not constantly there.

What follows is how to get some of it back.

Eat Food, Not Products

The useful distinction is not fat versus carbs. It is whether something is food or a product engineered to be eaten.

Meat, fish, eggs, vegetables, fruit, nuts, seeds, legumes if you tolerate them. Things that spoil. Things with one ingredient.

Whole food carries fiber, which feeds the bacteria that clear your estrogen. It does not spike insulin the way refined flour and sugar do — which, from Part Two, means it does not flood your mitochondria with more electrons than the chain can handle. And it arrives without the preservatives, industrial oils, and chemical residues from Part Four.

I am not naming a macro camp. Women thrive on very different ratios.

What to do

Read the ingredient list before the macros. If you cannot picture the plant or animal it came from, it is a product.

Protein And Muscle

Randomized trial · 2,437 women

Muscle is not vanity. It is where you dispose of glucose — less muscle means more circulating insulin doing the same job. It also predicts how well you would handle treatment if you ever needed it.

Most women eat less protein than they need, especially after fifty, when the body responds less readily to the same amount. Protein and resistance training work together — neither does the job alone.

There is trial evidence pointing the same direction. In a randomized study of 2,437 women with early-stage breast cancer, the group assigned to reduce dietary fat had 24% fewer relapses over five years. They also lost about six pounds. A parallel trial that increased vegetables and fruit but produced no weight change found nothing — which suggests the body composition change, rather than the specific foods, carried the effect.

What to do

Protein at every meal, not just dinner. Resistance training twice a week.

Movement Is The Strongest Evidence In This Book

Meta-analysis · 151 cohorts

Of everything in these pages, nothing has more human data behind it than physical activity — and it is routinely mentioned in passing rather than treated as what it is.

A meta-analysis of studies following women after a breast cancer diagnosis found that those who were physically active had:

34%
lower breast cancer mortality
41%
lower all-cause mortality
24%
lower risk of recurrence

A larger analysis pooling 151 cohorts and nearly 1.5 million cancer patients found the greatest benefit of any cancer type was in breast cancer.

How much, actually

This is the part that changes what people do. Meeting standard activity guidelines — about 2.5 hours a week — was associated with roughly a 50% reduction in all-cause mortality. But the curve starts long before that: one hour a week was associated with about a 25% reduction.

In one analysis, five-year mortality was 11% for women doing no meaningful exercise, 4% for those doing some but not enough, and 3% for those meeting guidelines.

Most of the benefit arrives early. You do not have to become an athlete to collect it.
Go deeper: why it works

Why it works reaches nearly every mechanism in Part Two. Exercise lowers insulin and IGF-1. It builds the muscle that disposes of glucose. It reduces body fat, which lowers aromatase activity. It supports the natural killer cells that chronic stress suppresses. It lowers inflammation. And it is the single strongest known stimulus for growing new mitochondria — the one thing on this list that directly rebuilds what sugar and time have damaged.

What to do

Resistance training twice a week for muscle. Walking most days for insulin. Ten to twenty minutes after dinner blunts the glucose rise more than the same walk earlier. If you are starting from nothing, start with the hour — it is where the steepest part of the curve is.

Let Your Body Finish Digesting

2,413 women · 30,000 dietary records

Researchers followed 2,413 women with early-stage breast cancer, analyzing roughly 30,000 time-stamped dietary records.

36%
higher risk of recurrence in women who fasted fewer than 13 hours overnight, compared with women who fasted 13 hours or longer

The average woman in the study fasted 12.5 hours. The gap between the groups was small on a clock and large in outcome.

The longer fasters also had lower HbA1c — a measure of average blood sugar over about three months — and slept longer. Body weight and inflammation markers did not explain the difference. Glucose regulation and sleep did.

Finish dinner at 7:00. Eat at 8:00. That is the entire intervention.

Thirteen hours is not a diet. It is the gap your great-grandmother had without trying.
What to do

Thirteen hours from your last bite to your first. Most of it happens while you are asleep.

What The Gap Does Inside You

When you stop eating, things shift within hours. Insulin falls. IGF-1 comes down. Your body reaches for stored fat. AMPK, an energy sensor that switches on when fuel runs low, activates. mTOR, a growth pathway that tells cells to build and divide, quiets.

Those are the growth signals from Part Two, and they respond to an overnight gap. That is what the recurrence study was measuring — the researchers found the effect ran through glucose regulation and sleep, not through weight or inflammation.

A Note On Autophagy, Because You Will Hear About It

Autophagy means self-eating — cells breaking down damaged proteins and worn-out mitochondria instead of letting them accumulate. You will see charts online promising it switches on at a specific hour of fasting.

Go deeper: what the human studies show

Those charts come from rodents. In humans, autophagy is extremely difficult to measure, because it requires tissue biopsy rather than a blood test. What human studies exist are modest and inconsistent: at 36 hours of fasting the effect on muscle was small and appeared only in untrained people, and one 2024 study measuring it directly in human tissue found no difference between overnight fasting and after eating at all.

So the thirteen-hour gap is not an autophagy protocol, and it does not need to be. The insulin and IGF-1 changes are real, measurable, and happen within hours — and those are what the recurrence data actually tracked.

You cannot buy any of this. You can only make room for it by not eating for a while.

Fasting During Treatment

Randomized trial · 131 women

A randomized trial called DIRECT studied 131 women with HER2-negative stage II or III breast cancer. Women assigned a fasting-mimicking diet around their chemotherapy were more likely to show a complete or partial tumor response, and had less chemotherapy-related DNA damage in their immune cells.

In hormone-receptor-positive models, fasting-mimicking diets enhanced the effects of tamoxifen and fulvestrant, lowering insulin, IGF-1, leptin, and growth signaling — with similar metabolic shifts measured in human patients.

Notice the scale. DIRECT enrolled 131 women. A drug with this signal would already be in a multi-thousand-patient trial. Fasting has no patent holder, so there is no one to fund it.

Ask your team

If you are in active treatment, coordinate timing with your oncology team — unintentional weight loss during chemotherapy works against you.

Ketogenic Approaches

Randomized trial · 80 women

A ketogenic diet restricts carbohydrate enough that your body begins producing ketones and running largely on fat. It lowers glucose and insulin, reduces IGF-1 signaling, and changes the fuel available to tumors.

A randomized trial of 80 women with locally advanced and metastatic breast cancer put half on a ketogenic diet alongside chemotherapy for 12 weeks. Tumors shrank by 27mm in the ketogenic group versus 6mm in the control group, and stage decreased significantly in locally advanced patients. Insulin and the inflammatory marker TNF-α both fell. There was no difference in metastatic disease.

A well-formulated version means adequate quality protein, non-starchy vegetables, olive oil, avocado, nuts and seeds, fatty fish, and attention to electrolytes. Not unlimited processed meat and cheese.

Ask your team

If you take glucose-lowering medication, your doctor needs to adjust it before you start.

Sunlight

Women with higher vitamin D levels have roughly 15% lower breast cancer risk. Vitamin D regulates cell growth, supports DNA repair, and drives immune function — including the natural killer cells from Part Two that clear abnormal cells.

In a randomized trial of 25,871 people, vitamin D supplementation reduced advanced cancer — cancer that had spread or proved fatal — by 17%. Among participants at a normal weight the reduction was 38%. There was no benefit in those who were overweight or obese, which is its own argument for addressing body composition first.

But vitamin D is one output of sun exposure, not the whole of it. In a 20-year study of 29,518 Swedish women, those who avoided the sun had roughly double the all-cause mortality of women with the highest sun exposure. The researchers noted the effect size sat in the same range as smoking or inactivity.

That mortality advantage came mostly from lower cardiovascular and non-cancer death rather than lower cancer death specifically. But it directly undercuts the advice most women have been given their entire lives.

Morning light does something separate again. It sets the clock governing hormone release, DNA repair timing, cortisol rhythm, and metabolism — the mechanism underneath both the shift-work and the cortisol findings in Part Three.

What to do

Outside within an hour of waking, ideally without sunglasses. Sun on skin when the season allows, without burning. Ask for a 25(OH)D level at your next blood draw so you know where you stand.

Sleep In The Dark

Melatonin is an antioxidant with a role in DNA repair, and light at night suppresses it. Your deepest repair work happens in darkness.

Go deeper: the two melatonin systems

There are actually two melatonin systems, and they respond to opposite signals. The familiar one comes from the pineal gland, is released in darkness, and is shut down by light. A second, emerging area of research describes melatonin produced inside your cells’ mitochondria in response to the near-infrared wavelengths in sunlight — which pass deep into tissue in a way visible light does not.

If that holds up, the practical conclusion is the one you would reach anyway: get sun during the day and darkness at night. They are not in conflict.

Melatonin as a treatment

Separately, melatonin has been studied as a supplement alongside cancer treatment, at doses far higher than anything sold for sleep. Across 21 trials in solid tumors, pooled one-year mortality was 37% lower in patients receiving melatonin. Combined with chemotherapy, complete tumor response was about 2.5 times more likely, and patients had less fatigue, less neurotoxicity, and fewer blood count problems.

In a small breast cancer study, women whose disease was progressing on tamoxifen were given 20mg of melatonin each evening alongside it. Four of fourteen had a partial response, and IGF-1 fell significantly — more steeply in the women who responded.

Ask your team

Twenty milligrams is a pharmacological dose, roughly ten to forty times what is sold for sleep, and it belongs in a conversation with your oncologist rather than a supplement aisle.

What to do

Dark bedroom — genuinely dark. Dimmer, warmer light after sunset. Consistent sleep and wake times, weekends included.

What This Actually Looks Like

Not a protocol. A direction.

The whole chapter, in one place

Food that spoils. Protein at every meal. Resistance training twice a week and a walk most days. Thirteen hours from last bite to first. Light in the morning, darkness at night. Water from glass. Food heated in ceramic. Alcohol occasional rather than daily.

None of it is exotic. Most of it is what your great-grandmother did without thinking about it.

She lived in an environment that made it automatic. You live in one engineered to make it hard.

Which means for you it has to be chosen — repeatedly, imperfectly, over years. That is not a burden. That is the part that is actually in your hands.
