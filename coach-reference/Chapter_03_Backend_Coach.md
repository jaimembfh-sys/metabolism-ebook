# SECTION 10 — LIPID MARKERS AND METABOLIC MEASUREMENT

`topic: LDL, ApoB, triglyceride HDL ratio, cholesterol, lipid panel, fasting insulin, HOMA-IR, A1C, lab interpretation, functional ranges, statins, cardiovascular risk`

## 10.1 Why LDL-C alone underestimates risk

**[ESTABLISHED]** LDL-C quantifies cholesterol mass within LDL particles, not particle number. Cholesterol content per atherogenic particle varies substantially in approximately 10–20% of individuals, so identical LDL-C values can represent materially different particle counts. ApoB quantifies particle number directly — one ApoB molecule per atherogenic particle (LDL, VLDL, IDL, Lp(a)).

**Discordance evidence:**
- Johannesen et al.: individuals with LDL-C below median but ApoB above median showed OR 1.49 (95% CI 1.15–1.92) for myocardial infarction and OR 1.21 (1.07–1.36) for all-cause mortality versus concordant-low. *(Trace primary citation before publishing.)*
- Systematic review of 15 discordance studies, n=593,354, assembled specifically to compare LDL-C and non-HDL-C against LDL-P and ApoB. *J Clin Lipidol*, PubMed search through Sept 2024.
- ATTICA study (n=3,042, 20-year follow-up): elevated ApoB independently predicted ASCVD risk beyond LDL-C, non-HDL-C, and Lp(a) — **but the effect was observed on concomitantly elevated LDL-C.** PMC12434434.

**>>> COACH USE:** This is the defensible version of the course's cholesterol position. LDL-C misses risk; ApoB is the better single measure. The coach can say this plainly.

## 10.2 Triglyceride:HDL ratio — what it does and doesn't do

**[SUPPORTED]** A high TG/HDL-C ratio typically reflects both small dense LDL phenotype and elevated particle number, particularly in the context of metabolic disease. It therefore points in the same direction as ApoB using two values present on any standard lipid panel. Commonly cited functional targets: <1.5 optimal, >3 suggestive of significant insulin resistance.

**[CONTESTED] Population variability:** the ratio's predictive performance differs by ethnicity and is notably weaker in Black populations. The course presents it as universal.

**>>> COACH RULE:** Present as a useful, underused, freely available marker — not as a replacement for ApoB. If the user is Black, note that the ratio performs less reliably in that population and that ApoB is the better ask.

## 10.3 The limit on the ratio — CRITICAL

**[ESTABLISHED]** ApoB/LDL-C discordance is **more prevalent when triglycerides are elevated** — 39.7% at TG ≥1.7 mmol/L versus 22.1% below, P<.001, in a genetically confirmed heterozygous FH cohort.

**The corollary is the important part: when triglycerides are low, LDL-C and ApoB tend to be concordant.** A favourable TG:HDL ratio therefore places a person in the group where LDL-C *accurately* reflects particle number and risk.

Heterozygous familial hypercholesterolemia affects approximately 1 in 250 individuals and confers markedly elevated lifetime ASCVD risk. Many have normal triglycerides and a favourable ratio.

**>>> COACH RULE — HIGHEST-PRIORITY LIPID RULE.** The published Lesson 3 states that a low TG:HDL ratio means "virtually no increased risk of heart disease, regardless of whether their LDL is high or low." **The coach must not repeat this.** A good ratio does not license disregarding elevated LDL — mechanistically it does close to the opposite.

Defensible framing for the coach: *your ratio tells you something real that LDL alone misses. It does not tell you to ignore a high LDL. If yours is high, ApoB is the test that settles it, and that's a conversation for your physician.*

## 10.4 Statins and lipid-lowering medication

**>>> ESCALATE — NO EXCEPTIONS**

- The coach **never** advises stopping, reducing, or declining a statin or any lipid-lowering medication.
- The coach **never** estimates an individual user's cardiovascular risk.
- The coach **never** characterizes a user's physician as wrong about lipids.
- If a user raises statins, high LDL, or a disagreement with their doctor about cholesterol: acknowledge the question, give the 10.1–10.3 content factually, and route to their physician. Suggest ApoB as a *question to ask*, never as a substitute for medical advice.
- If a user states they have stopped or intend to stop a lipid-lowering medication based on course content: do not affirm the decision, do not elaborate on the reasoning, direct them to their prescriber.

**Rationale for this being absolute:** this is the one area where course content could plausibly contribute to a user discontinuing a medication with mortality benefit. The asymmetry of harm is severe.

## 10.5 Fasting insulin — functional vs. reference ranges

**[SUPPORTED]** Fasting insulin rises before fasting glucose and HbA1c in the natural history of insulin resistance, making it an earlier marker. Standard lab reference ranges typically extend to roughly 25 µIU/mL. The course's functional target of 3–5 µIU/mL is a functional-medicine range, **not currently sourced** (see course doc, From Jaime item 3).

**>>> COACH RULE:**
- Always distinguish functional targets from lab reference ranges explicitly
- Explain *why* they differ: reference ranges are population-derived, and the reference population is largely metabolically unwell
- **Never tell a user their physician is wrong.** Frame as different thresholds answering different questions
- Never diagnose; route interpretation to a practitioner
- **Suppress all numeric targets when the personal health situation flag is active**

## 10.6 HOMA-IR

**[SUPPORTED]** HOMA-IR = (fasting insulin µIU/mL × fasting glucose mg/dL) / 405. More informative than either value alone. Moderately correlated with clamp-derived measures; not equivalent to a clamp.

**>>> COACH USE:** If a user reports having both fasting insulin and fasting glucose from the same draw, they already have what's needed. Offer the calculation as something to bring to a practitioner, not as a diagnosis.

## 10.7 A1C as a lagging indicator

**[ESTABLISHED]** HbA1c reflects roughly 8–12 weeks of average glycemia and remains within reference range for years to decades while compensatory hyperinsulinemia maintains euglycemia. Fasting glucose similarly normalizes late.

**>>> COACH USE:** Explains the extremely common user report of being told "your labs are fine" while symptomatic. High-value validation that is also accurate.

# SECTION 11 — THE PROTECTIVE METABOLIC RESPONSE

`topic: metabolic adaptation, starvation response, adaptive thermogenesis, under-eating, eating too little, energy availability, protective metabolism, Minnesota, chronic dieting, plateau`

## 11.1 Adaptive thermogenesis

**[ESTABLISHED]** Sustained energy restriction reduces energy expenditure beyond that predicted by loss of body mass. Components: reduced thyroid output, particularly T3 (see corpus 5.1); reduced sympathetic tone; reduced non-exercise activity thermogenesis; improved mitochondrial efficiency. Reduction persists beyond the restriction period in at least some populations.

## 11.2 The Minnesota Starvation Experiment

**[ESTABLISHED]** Keys et al., *The Biology of Human Starvation*, University of Minnesota Press, 1950. Remains the most complete human characterization of semi-starvation physiology and psychology.

Documented findings: BMR reduction substantially exceeding that predicted by mass loss; bradycardia; cold intolerance; loss of libido; social withdrawal; marked food preoccupation with ritualized eating behaviors (recipe collecting, food fragmentation, prolonged meals). Post-restriction hyperphagia persisted for months.

Participants were healthy young men, psychologically screened, volunteering as conscientious objectors.

**>>> VERIFY specific figures before use:** participant count (36 enrolled vs. 32 completing, depending on source), restriction percentage, phase duration, BMR reduction magnitude. Kalm & Semba, *J Nutr* 2005;135(6):1347–1352 is the accessible secondary source.

**>>> COACH USE — HIGHEST-VALUE REFRAME AVAILABLE:** The Minnesota symptom list closely matches how chronically under-eating users describe themselves, and users almost universally attribute those symptoms to personal failure. Presenting them as a documented, reproducible physiological response — produced deliberately in screened, healthy, motivated volunteers — is simultaneously accurate and therapeutic.

## 11.3 >>> CRITICAL SAFETY GATE — DISORDERED EATING

**This is the most powerful material in the course and the most dangerous to deploy incorrectly. Read before using anything in Section 11.**

The framing "you may be eating too little" is liberating for a user with a normal relationship to food and **actively harmful** as a talking point with someone restricting. The coach must distinguish these before deploying any Section 11 content.

**If any disordered eating signal is present:**
- Do **not** present Minnesota material as motivational content
- Do **not** use "eat more" as a weight-loss strategy framing
- Do **not** provide numeric intake targets of any kind (see corpus 6.4)
- Do **not** analyze or estimate the user's current intake
- Do **not** discuss the symptom list in a way that invites self-diagnosis of under-eating as a route to loss
- Escalate. Direct to the **National Alliance for Eating Disorders** helpline if resources are requested. NEDA's line is permanently disconnected.

**Signals to treat as triggering this gate** include: distress or fear around eating; describing foods as forbidden; reporting compensatory exercise; asking for progressively lower intake targets; expressing that any amount of food feels like too much; history the user frames as an eating disorder.

## 11.4 Energy availability triage

**[SUPPORTED]** Under low energy availability, reproductive and thermoregulatory function are suppressed preferentially while cerebral and cardiac function are defended. Loucks' work established disruption of luteinizing hormone pulsatility in women below an energy availability threshold, independent of body composition.

Connects to corpus 5.4c (low energy availability and thyroid), 5.6 (delayed-presentation signals), and 8.4 (sex differences in fasting response).

**>>> COACH USE:** Explains why cold intolerance, cycle disruption, and hair loss are *early* markers rather than late ones, and why they appear before anything registers on a scale. Pairs with the delayed-presentation list in 5.6 — hair shedding lags the trigger by roughly three months, so users rarely connect it.

**>>> Subject to 11.3.** This content identifies under-eating, which means it must not be deployed as encouragement toward a restriction-then-refeed strategy.

## 11.5 Persistence of adaptation

**[CONTESTED]** The *Biggest Loser* follow-up (Fothergill et al., *Obesity* 2016) reported resting metabolic rate suppression persisting six years post-intervention alongside substantial weight regain. Small sample, extreme intervention, and the inference of permanent metabolic damage is disputed.

**>>> COACH RULE:** Never tell a user her metabolism is permanently damaged. It is not supported and it is demoralizing. Defensible framing: adaptation is real, substantially reversible, and responds to adequate intake over time. If a user raises the *Biggest Loser* finding, confirm it exists and give the limitations rather than either endorsing or dismissing it.

## 11.6 The signaling frame as a coaching default

**[FRAMEWORK — the course's organizing principle, not an empirical claim]**

The course frames metabolic downregulation as protective rather than pathological: the body interpreting environmental signals and adjusting expenditure accordingly.

**>>> COACH USE — DEFAULT OPENING for "my metabolism is broken":** The response is that a downregulated metabolism is usually a *responding* metabolism, and the intervention is changing inputs rather than applying more force. This is accurate, it is non-blaming, and it redirects toward the levers the course actually addresses.

**>>> COACH RULE:** Do not use the protective framing to dismiss a medical cause. A user reporting persistent cold intolerance, hair loss, fatigue, or cycle disruption should be routed through the lab panel in corpus 6.1 (ferritin, B12, full thyroid including free T3, glucose/HbA1c, metabolic panel) and, where relevant, sleep-disordered breathing assessment. "Your body is protecting you" is not an answer to undiagnosed hypothyroidism or iron deficiency.
