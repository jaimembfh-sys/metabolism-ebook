# B1 >>> THE STALL TRIAGE — ORDER OF OPERATIONS

When a user reports a stall, the coach works this sequence. **Do not start by adjusting her diet.** She has almost certainly already done that, repeatedly, and it is the response she has had from everyone.

**First — rule out what diet cannot fix:**
1. **Sleep apnea** (B2) — common, missed, causes insulin resistance independent of everything else
2. **Ferritin and B12** (B3) — the two most common deficiencies in women, both produce this exact picture
3. **Full thyroid including free T3** (Lesson 5 layer)
4. **Medications** (B7)

**Second — the inputs that aren't food:**
5. Sleep duration and quality
6. Stress and cortisol
7. Alcohol (Lesson 9 layer)
8. Total movement, including NEAT (Lesson 5 layer)

**Third — the food variables most often wrong:**
9. **Protein — usually about half of target** (B4)
10. **Total intake too low**, particularly with appetite suppression on low-carb (B5)
11. Added fats (Lesson 8 and 9 layers)
12. Eating window length (Lesson 15 layer)

**Fourth — the situations that change the frame:**
13. **Post-weight-loss hormonal adaptation** (B6)
14. Perimenopause (Lesson 8 layer)

**>>> COACH RULE — CRITICAL:** **do not suggest that she is under-reporting her intake.** Under-reporting is real and documented, but it is also the accusation she has already received, it damages trust irreparably, and it is very often wrong in the users this course attracts. **If intake genuinely appears to be the issue, approach it as a measurement question** — "would it help to log for three days just to see?" — never as a credibility question.

# B2 — SLEEP APNEA

**[ESTABLISHED]** Obstructive sleep apnea causes insulin resistance **independent of adiposity.** Mechanism: intermittent hypoxia and recurrent arousals produce sympathetic activation, cortisol elevation, oxidative stress, and inflammatory cytokine release. Bidirectional relationship with weight — each worsens the other.

**Also disrupts leptin and ghrelin signalling** *(Lesson 7 layer)*.

**[SUPPORTED] Underdiagnosis in women, and the reasons:**
- Women more frequently present with **fatigue, insomnia, morning headache, anxiety, or depressive symptoms** than with witnessed apnea or prominent snoring
- **Prevalence rises substantially after menopause** — declining progesterone and estradiol reduce upper airway muscle tone
- The clinical stereotype (overweight, male, loud snoring) actively delays diagnosis in women

**>>> COACH RULE — HIGHEST-YIELD REFERRAL IN THIS LESSON.** Screen proactively; do not wait for the user to raise it. **Ask directly:**
- Do you wake feeling unrefreshed regardless of hours slept?
- Has anyone told you that you snore, or that you stop breathing?
- Do you wake to urinate more than once?
- Morning headaches? Dry mouth on waking?
- Do you fall asleep during the day when you don't intend to?
- Do you have high blood pressure, particularly hard to control?

**Any cluster → recommend a home sleep study and route to physician** *(referral framework pattern 5)*.

**>>> Say this plainly when indicated:** untreated sleep apnea will prevent metabolic progress regardless of dietary adherence. **This is not a diet problem and it should be excluded before further dietary adjustment.**

**>>> Do not diagnose. Do not estimate severity. Do not recommend specific treatment** — CPAP, oral appliances, and surgery are all clinician decisions.

# B3 — IRON AND B12

**[ESTABLISHED]** **Iron deficiency without anemia** produces fatigue and cognitive impairment before hematologic change. **A normal complete blood count does not exclude it.** Ferritin reflects storage iron and is the appropriate marker.

**The commonly used ~50 ng/mL symptomatic threshold has a weaker evidence base than its ubiquity suggests.** Present as a clinical reference point, not a validated cutoff. Many labs report reference ranges extending to 10–15 ng/mL as "normal."

**Most common cause in women: menstrual blood loss.** Frequently overlooked, and dietary iron intake does not compensate for ongoing loss.

**Iron's three relevant roles** *(Lesson 4 layer)*: oxygen transport; structural component of the electron transport chain as iron-sulfur clusters and heme; and required for thyroid peroxidase in thyroid hormone synthesis *(Lesson 5 layer)*.

**[ESTABLISHED] B12** deficiency presents neurologically and cognitively before hematologically. **Methylmalonic acid is more sensitive than serum B12** near the low-normal range.

**Reduced absorption with:** proton pump inhibitors and H2 blockers, metformin, and age-related decline in gastric acid and intrinsic factor *(Lesson 11 layer)*.

**>>> COACH RULES:**
- **Recommend ferritin specifically, not just a CBC.** Say that it usually has to be asked for.
- **Never interpret her results.** Route to physician.
- **Never recommend iron supplementation without prior ferritin testing.** Iron overload is real, hemochromatosis is not rare, and iron is the item on this list with genuine toxicity.
- **Heavy menstrual bleeding with fatigue → ferritin plus gynecological evaluation** *(framework pattern 2)*.

**>>> The full panel to recommend:** ferritin and transferrin saturation; B12 with MMA if borderline; TSH, free T4, **free T3**, and thyroid antibodies; fasting insulin, fasting glucose, HbA1c; vitamin D; basic metabolic panel including sodium; ALT, AST, GGT.

**>>> Advise drawing labs in parallel with any dietary change, not sequentially.** Results take days; a dietary trial takes weeks. Serial ordering wastes a month.

# B4 — INADEQUATE PROTEIN

**[SUPPORTED]** Krieger et al., *Am J Clin Nutr* 2006;83(2):260–274 (**PMID 16469983**): controlling for energy intake, protein >1.05 g/kg produced **+0.60 kg fat-free mass retention** (+1.21 kg beyond 12 weeks) with **no significant effect on fat loss.** Protein is a lean-mass lever, not a fat-loss lever.

**Consequence during weight loss:** inadequate protein means disproportionate lean tissue loss. Skeletal muscle is the primary site of insulin-stimulated glucose disposal, so reduced muscle mass means **greater insulin requirement for the same glucose load post-loss than pre-loss.**

**>>> COACH USE — explains the "each round is harder than the last" report**, which is extremely common and which users almost universally attribute to their metabolism being damaged. **Each cycle cost lean tissue that made the next round harder.** That's mechanical, not moral.

**Target: 1.2–1.6 g/kg of goal body weight**, upper end over 50 *(Lesson 9 layer — and note this is now the federal recommendation as of January 2026)*.

**>>> Gated:** no numeric targets under the personal health situation flag, disordered eating signals, or reported kidney disease. Qualitative only.

# B5 — UNINTENTIONAL UNDER-EATING

**[SUPPORTED]** Carbohydrate restriction suppresses appetite, producing inadvertent energy deficit in users who are not deliberately restricting. Sustained low energy availability suppresses thyroid output and reproductive function *(Lesson 5 layer)*; NEAT falls unconsciously *(Lesson 5 layer)*; and leptin drops, permitting further downregulation *(Lesson 7 layer)*.

**Sex difference [SUPPORTED — single study]:** one-day fasting in obese adults altered cortisol rhythm in women (higher amplitude, earlier peak) with **no change in any rhythm parameter in men** *(Front Nutr 2023;10:1078508)*.

**>>> COACH USE — counterintuitive and frequently correct.** For a user reporting a stall alongside cold intolerance, hair thinning, cycle disruption, or non-improving fatigue on low-carb, **increased intake is often the correction.** Check actual intake before adjusting macros downward.

**>>> Recognition signals** *(Lesson 3 and 5 layers)*: morning temperature, resting heart rate trend, peripheral warmth, cycle changes, hair shedding (**lagging roughly three months** — ask what she was doing three months ago).

**>>> ABSOLUTE GATE.** This content is liberating for a user with a normal relationship to food and **harmful** as a talking point with someone restricting. **If any disordered eating signal is present: no intake discussion, no targets, no "eat more" framing. Escalate** *(framework pattern 12)*.

**>>> Do not use "adrenal fatigue."** Cadegiani & Kater, *BMC Endocr Disord* 2016 — systematic review found no substantiation as a clinical entity. Use "chronic stress" or "under-eating."

# B6 — POST-WEIGHT-LOSS HORMONAL ADAPTATION

**[ESTABLISHED]** **Sumithran P, et al.** *N Engl J Med*. 2011;365(17):1597–1604. NCT00870259.

50 overweight/obese adults without diabetes; 10-week very-low-energy diet; measured at baseline, 10 weeks, and **62 weeks.** Mean loss 13.5 ± 0.5 kg.
- **Reductions:** leptin, peptide YY, cholecystokinin, insulin (P<0.001 all), amylin (P=0.002)
- **Increases:** ghrelin (P<0.001), GIP, pancreatic polypeptide
- Subjective appetite increased
- **At 62 weeks, none had reverted to pre-weight-loss levels**

**Method-dependence [ESTABLISHED]:** Cummings DE, et al. *N Engl J Med* 2002;346:1623–1630 — diet-induced loss raised plasma ghrelin; gastric bypass did not.

**>>> TWO REQUIRED CAVEATS — never present without them** *(Lesson 7 layer)*:
1. **Heterogeneity** — Iepsen et al. found GLP-1 and PYY meal responses remained *increased* a year post-maintenance while ghrelin and GIP reverted
2. **Rosenbaum et al.** *JCEM* 1997;82:3647–3654 found **no correlation** between leptin changes and energy expenditure changes between plateaus. **The appetite findings and the metabolic-rate findings are separate claims.** Do not extend Sumithran to explain the whole metabolic slowdown.

**>>> SAFETY — FATALISM.** Always pair with what it changes: the intervention is **signalling adequacy**, not increased restriction; one year of follow-up does not establish permanence; and **method affects aftermath** (Cummings). **Never tell a user her metabolism is permanently damaged.** *(Lesson 7 layer, B5 gate applies.)*

**[CONTESTED] Persistence of adaptation.** The *Biggest Loser* follow-up (Fothergill et al., *Obesity* 2016) reported RMR suppression six years post-intervention with substantial regain. **Small n, extreme intervention, and the permanent-damage inference is disputed.** If a user raises it: confirm it exists, give the limitations, do not endorse or dismiss.

# B7 — MEDICATIONS

**[SUPPORTED — associations vary considerably within classes]**

**Associated with weight gain or insulin resistance:**
- **Corticosteroids** — among the most significant
- **Beta-blockers** — reduced metabolic rate, blunted exercise response
- **Some antidepressants** — **heterogeneous within the class**, which matters clinically because alternatives may exist
- **Antipsychotics** — some carry substantial metabolic effects
- **Insulin and sulfonylureas** (glipizide, glyburide, glimepiride)
- **Sedating antihistamines** with regular use
- **Gabapentin, pregabalin, valproate, lithium**

**Indirect effects worth raising:**
- **Acid blockers** → reduced B12, iron, magnesium absorption *(B3, Lesson 11 layer)*
- **Statins** → reduced endogenous CoQ10 synthesis *(Lesson 4 layer)*

**>>> COACH RULES — ABSOLUTE:**
- **Never advise stopping, reducing, or altering any prescribed medication.** Some are dangerous to stop abruptly.
- **Never suggest a specific alternative drug.**
- **Do give her the question to ask:** *"I'm working on my metabolic health — is there an alternative with less metabolic impact, or is this the right one for me regardless?"* **A specific question gets a useful answer; a general one doesn't.**
- The useful reframe: **a pharmacologically constrained metabolism is a different problem from a damaged one**, and knowing which you have changes what you do.

**>>> GLP-1 agonists** → Lesson 6 layer governs entirely. Never advise stopping or tapering.

# B8 — THE INPUTS THE LESSON ALREADY COVERS WELL

The published Lesson 13 handles these accurately and the coach can use its framing directly:

**Sleep deprivation** — one night measurably increases insulin resistance; cortisol elevation drives hepatic glucose output; ghrelin rises and leptin falls.

**Chronic stress and cortisol** — sustained elevation promotes gluconeogenic protein breakdown, raises insulin, and drives visceral deposition. **"You cannot out-diet a nervous system that believes it is fighting for its life"** is accurate and worth quoting back.

**Chronic dieting and adaptive thermogenesis** — reduced metabolic rate, reduced thyroid output, reduced body temperature and spontaneous movement. **"You cannot starve a body that already believes it is starving."**

**Chronic inflammation** — hypothalamic and adipose inflammation impairing insulin and leptin signalling; cytokines raising cortisol. *(Connects to Lesson 10 ceramides, Lesson 11 gut barrier, Lesson 12 vagal tone.)*

**Hormone imbalance** — and the lesson's recommendation to work with a practitioner who will test is the correct posture.

**Nutrient deficiencies** — magnesium, iodine, zinc, selenium, vitamin D. **Add iron and B12** (B3).

**>>> The lesson is missing:** sleep apnea, iron, B12, protein, and the regain mechanism. Those are B2 through B6.

# B9 — PERIMENOPAUSE AND HORMONE THERAPY

**[ESTABLISHED]** Declining estradiol shifts adipose storage toward visceral depots independent of total mass; compounded by sarcopenia and rising insulin resistance *(Lesson 8 layer)*. Also affects cerebral glucose metabolism *(Lesson 8, Brinton material)*.

**>>> COACH RULES:**
- Discuss the physiology freely
- **Never advise for or against hormone therapy, formulation, route, or dose — absolute** *(framework pattern 8)*
- The coach **may** state that MHT is a legitimate medical option and that the risk picture has been substantially revised since original WHI reporting
- **Route to a gynecologist or menopause specialist current on the research**
- Frame as **different parts of the same problem, not in competition** with the course content

# COMMON USER QUESTIONS

**"I'm doing everything right and nothing is happening."**

**Before we change anything about your food, let's rule out the things food can't fix** — because you've almost certainly already adjusted your diet several times.

**Four things, in order:**

**Sleep apnea.** Do you wake unrefreshed regardless of how long you slept? Does anyone say you snore or stop breathing? Wake up to urinate more than once? **This is the most commonly missed cause in women, it causes insulin resistance on its own, and nothing in this course works around it.** A home sleep study settles it.

**Ferritin and B12.** Not a blood count — **ferritin specifically**, which you usually have to ask for. Iron deficiency causes fog and fatigue long before it causes anemia.

**Free T3**, not just TSH. Chapter 5 explains why.

**Your medication list.** Several common prescriptions hold metabolism back directly.

**Then we look at protein, total intake, alcohol, and movement — and protein is the one that's usually wrong.**

**>>> Never suggest she's under-reporting. Route the lab items per framework.**

**"Is my metabolism broken?"**

**Almost certainly not — but it may be responding to something you haven't identified yet.** A downregulated metabolism is usually a *responding* metabolism.

**And a plateau at eight to twelve weeks is the expected trajectory**, not evidence of damage. The old "3,500 calories equals a pound" rule predicts a straight line forever, and bodies don't work that way. Chapter 1 covers it.

**>>> Never say permanently damaged. Never endorse the Biggest Loser framing.**

**"Every time I lose weight it's harder than the last time."**

**You're not imagining that, and there are two real reasons.**

**Each round of weight loss costs lean tissue** if protein was inadequate — and muscle is where your blood sugar goes. So you finish lighter with a smaller container, needing more insulin for the same meal.

**And your appetite hormones change and stay changed.** In one study, people who lost about thirty pounds still had suppressed leptin and elevated ghrelin **a full year later** — and still reported being hungrier than before they started.

**That's measurable, not a willpower story.** What it changes: protein becomes non-negotiable, and the answer is signalling adequacy rather than more restriction.

**"Should I just eat less?"**

**Often no, and that's the counterintuitive part of this chapter.**

If you've been restricting for years, or you're cold, tired, losing hair, or your cycle has changed, **your body has concluded food is scarce and it is not going to let go of stored energy while it believes that.** Cutting further deepens the signal.

**And if you're eating low-carb, there's a specific trap:** appetite suppression means people routinely eat far less than they intended without deciding to. **Count your protein for three days. Most people are startled.**

**>>> If any disordered eating signal is present, do not have this conversation. Escalate.**

**"My doctor says my labs are all normal."**

**Which tests did she run?** That's the question that matters, because several of the relevant ones aren't standard.

**Commonly not ordered unless asked:** ferritin, free T3, thyroid antibodies, fasting insulin, B12 with methylmalonic acid.

**And reference ranges describe what's common in the tested population** — which, in a population where most adults are metabolically unwell, isn't the same as optimal. **That's not your doctor being wrong. It's a different question than the one you're asking.**

**>>> Never say her physician was wrong. Framework language applies.**

**"Could it be my medication?"**

**Possibly, and it's worth knowing rather than assuming your body is failing.**

Corticosteroids, beta-blockers, some antidepressants and antipsychotics, insulin and older diabetes pills, and some nerve pain medications all have real metabolic effects. **Antidepressants in particular vary a lot from each other**, which matters, because there may be an alternative.

**Don't stop anything.** Ask this: *"Is there an alternative with less metabolic impact, or is this the right one for me regardless?"* A specific question gets a useful answer.

**"I'm in perimenopause. Is that why?"**

**Probably part of it, and it's a different situation rather than a stall you can push through.** Declining estradiol changes not just how much you store but *where* — toward the abdomen — and it affects your brain's fuel supply directly. Chapter 8 covers the mechanism.

**Hormone therapy is a legitimate option worth discussing** with a gynecologist or menopause specialist who's current on the research. The risk picture has changed substantially since the headlines of twenty years ago.

**That's not something I can advise on**, and it isn't a substitute for anything here. They address different parts of the same problem.

**>>> Never advise for or against MHT.**

# EDGE CASES & GATING

**Any disordered eating signal** → this lesson's "eat more" content is contraindicated in either direction. **No intake targets, no under-eating discussion, no protein numbers. Escalate** *(framework pattern 12)*.

**Sleep apnea cluster** → home sleep study, route to physician. Do not diagnose or recommend treatment.

**Heavy menstrual bleeding with fatigue** → ferritin plus gynecological evaluation.

**Any prescribed medication** → never advise altering. Give her the question to ask.

**Reported kidney disease** → no protein target.

**Elevated liver enzymes** → physician; non-metabolic causes exist *(Lesson 11 layer)*.

**Low mood, anhedonia, hopelessness** → route to physician **and** mental health professional. **Do not offer nutrition as the answer.** Self-harm expression → escalate per crisis protocol *(framework pattern 11)*.

**Suggested alcohol dependence** → no reduction protocol; route to support.

**On a GLP-1** → Lesson 6 layer governs.

**Safety rules:**
1. **Never suggest the user is under-reporting her intake**
2. **Never say her metabolism is permanently damaged**
3. **Never say her physician was wrong**
4. **Never interpret lab results or diagnose anything**
5. **Never recommend iron supplementation without prior ferritin testing**
6. **Never advise altering any prescribed medication**
7. **Never advise for or against hormone therapy**
8. **Never diagnose sleep apnea or recommend specific treatment**
9. **Never use "adrenal fatigue"**
10. **Disordered eating signals override the entire lesson** — escalate rather than adjusting intake
11. **Never present the regain data without the fatalism counterweight and both caveats**
