# WHAT THE LESSON TEACHES

1. **Time-restricted eating** lowers insulin exposure — condensing to 8–10 hours, fasting 14–16
2. **But fasting can be a stressor** for chronic fatigue, autoimmune illness, or a history of severe dieting
3. **The Rule of 3s** — three balanced meals (roughly ⅓ protein, ⅓ safe starches, ⅓ non-starchy vegetables), no snacking
4. **Morning insulin sensitivity is highest**; eat larger meals earlier; finish 2–3 hours before bed
5. **Cell clocks** exist in liver, muscle, fat, and pancreas, governing tissue-specific insulin sensitivity
6. **One meal a day burns up to 38% more energy** — *this is incorrect; see B1*
7. **Do not fear salt** — low insulin causes sodium excretion, and the hormonal response to replacing it promotes fat cell growth
8. **Cut snacks** — continuous insulin plus mitochondria needing maintenance breaks
9. **Nutrient density** — ruminant meats, eggs, fatty fish, organ meats, fibrous vegetables
10. **Sourcing matters** — grass-fed and grass-finished, pasture-raised, wild-caught, organic for skin-on produce, filtered water
11. **(New)** The nutrient sufficiency gap; the eating window statistic; autophagy; fasting and cortisol

# B1 >>> THE 38% CLAIM — DO NOT REPEAT

**[REFUTED — the only genuine factual error in the course]**

**Source:** Tai MM, Castillo P, Pi-Sunyer FX. *Am J Clin Nutr*. 1991;54(5):783–787. **PMID 1951147.** Seven healthy normal-weight young women; identical 750 kcal meal consumed over 10 minutes vs. six 125-kcal portions at 30-minute intervals; **metabolic rate measured for 5 hours from meal start.** TEF significantly higher on the large-meal day (P<0.05).

**Why it doesn't support the claim:**

1. **n=7**, single crossover, one population
2. **Measurement window artifact.** The final small portion was consumed at 2.5 hours, so roughly half its thermic response fell outside the 5-hour window. The large meal's full response fit inside; the small meals' did not. **A later study with a longer window found no difference** in total 10-hour TEF between 2 large and 4 small meals (43.43 ± 5.01 vs 43.42 ± 4.72 L O₂), explicitly attributing the null to more complete measurement
3. **Whole-room calorimetry contradicts it.** A 3 vs 6 meals/day crossover found no differences in 24-hour energy expenditure, RQ, or fat oxidation
4. **Internal inconsistency.** The same lesson's "Cut the Snacks" section argues against frequent eating. **If frequent meals don't lower metabolic rate, infrequent meals don't raise it.** Total thermic cost tracks the food, not the schedule

**[SUPPORTED — the finding that does hold]** In the 3-vs-6 meal comparison, participants eating 6 meals reported **greater hunger and desire to eat.** **Fewer meals win on appetite, not thermogenesis.** Connects to Lesson 7 ghrelin entrainment.

**[SUPPORTED] Circadian TEF.** Identical meals produce a greater thermic response in the morning than the evening. **Caveat:** some of the apparent daily TEF rhythm is attributable to the circadian rhythm in resting metabolic rate depending on calculation method (*JCEM* 2022;107(2):e708). **Directionally sound; do not over-quantify.**

**>>> COACH RULE:** never state or confirm the 38% figure. **It appears twice in the published lesson, including a Key Insight box.** If a user cites it, correct gently and give the appetite finding, which supports the same practical conclusion.

# B2 — TIME-RESTRICTED EATING AND HABITUAL WINDOWS

**[ESTABLISHED]** **Gill S, Panda S.** *Cell Metab*. 2015 — in healthy adults tracked by smartphone app, **approximately 50% ate within a window greater than ~14.75 hours; only 10% ate within 12 hours or less.**

**>>> COACH USE — HIGHEST PRACTICAL VALUE IN THIS LAYER.** Most users are not choosing between an 8-hour and a 10-hour window. **They are eating across roughly 15 hours without realizing it**, because coffee with cream, a bite while cooking, and evening snacks don't register as meals.

**Recommended first action: three days of logging every caloric intake including standing-at-the-counter bites**, before any window prescription. **Then narrow to 12 hours.** Meaningful for the large majority and requires no precision.

**>>> This reframes TRE from optimization to awareness**, which is more achievable and reduces the risk of triggering over-restriction.

**[REFUTED] Personalized clock-time windows.** Cortisol's diurnal rhythm is generated by SCN projections to the hypothalamic paraventricular nucleus, plus autonomic signals and local adrenal clocks. **In humans the SCN is entrained primarily by light, not food.** There is no published support for a window calculable from a questionnaire, for 90-minute precision determining fat storage, or for protocols "calibrated to a menopausal hormonal profile."

**>>> This claim set is actively marketed to women over 45** via advertorials with fabricated or misattributed citations. If a user raises such a product: **be factual and non-condescending.** Confirm the underlying frustration is legitimate, explain what the research supports, and **do not mock her for having believed it** — these are professionally produced.

# B3 — FASTING AND CORTISOL

**[ESTABLISHED]** The HPA axis is activated by fasting; cortisol stimulates hepatic gluconeogenesis as a necessary counter-regulatory response.

- **Meta-analysis:** acutely elevated plasma cortisol following fasting, **but not with less severe calorie restriction** — severity-dependent
- **10-day zero-calorie fast:** cortisol rose during the first five days (Steinhauser 2018)
- **72-hour fast in healthy men:** 24-hour mean cortisol significantly higher than after a weight-maintenance diet (Chan 2003)
- **5-day fasting** increases cortisol and **shifts the peak from morning to afternoon**
- **Early TRF (8am–2pm) for 4 days** slightly but significantly **increased** morning serum cortisol

**[SUPPORTED — single study] Sex difference.** One-day fasting in obese adults: female participants showed altered cortisol rhythm (higher amplitude, earlier acrophase); **no rhythm parameters changed in males** (*Front Nutr* 2023;10:1078508).

**[CONTESTED] Longer-term TRE is less clear.** One review distinguishes non-restrictive TRE (appears to lower cortisol) from isocaloric TRE (raises it), suggesting **total intake matters more than the window.**

**>>> COACH RULE — CRITICAL:** if a user asks about fasting to lower cortisol, **the answer is that fasting raises it.** The correct response to elevated cortisol from over-restriction is **less restriction, not a longer fast.** Users arriving from "cortisol reset" marketing will have the opposite expectation.

**>>> This is the mechanism behind the lesson's Rule of 3s**, which is a genuinely differentiated and correct recommendation. **Support it actively.**

**>>> DEFAULT for uncertain users: recommend the Rule of 3s first.** It provides low-insulin inter-meal windows without adding a famine signal. **Narrowing later is easy; recovering from months of counterproductive fasting is not.**

# B4 — SALT AND THE ADIPOSE RAAS

**[ESTABLISHED]** Insulin promotes renal sodium reabsorption. Reduced insulin increases natriuresis and fluid loss — the mechanism behind early rapid weight loss on carbohydrate restriction and behind "keto flu" symptoms.

**>>> CALIBRATION:** the natriuresis is **largely front-loaded to the first one to two weeks**; renal adaptation follows. **Do not assume persisting sodium depletion in a long-term low-carbohydrate eater who salts to taste** *(Lesson 8 layer)*.

**[SUPPORTED] Sodium restriction activates the RAAS with metabolic consequences** — raising plasma triglycerides and total cholesterol, causing insulin resistance, and stimulating both RAAS and the sympathetic nervous system.

**[SUPPORTED — and the lesson has this backwards] The two hormones act oppositely on adipogenesis:**
- **Aldosterone**, via the mineralocorticoid receptor, **promotes** adipogenesis, adipose expansion, and inflammation
- **Angiotensin II**, via AT1, **inhibits** adipocyte differentiation — producing a greater proportion of **large, insulin-resistant adipocytes** and ectopic lipid deposition. AT1 blockade improves insulin sensitivity **with enhanced differentiation into small insulin-sensitive cells**

**>>> This is the Lesson 9 hyperplasia/hypertrophy framework arriving from a different direction.** Angiotensin II blocking new fat cell formation *is* the hypertrophy pathway. **The corrected version is a stronger argument than the published one.**

**[ANIMAL DATA] Low-sodium diet increased inflammation and visceral adiposity** in LDL-receptor-knockout mice (PMC11432465).

**>>> The published lesson says "clinical biochemical data reveals."** That overstates it — **the direct sodium-to-adiposity evidence is in genetically modified mice.** The coach should present the fat cell consequence as a well-supported inference, not a demonstrated human outcome.

**>>> ABSOLUTE SAFETY RULE:** **never recommend liberal sodium intake to a user reporting hypertension, kidney disease, heart failure, or taking antihypertensives** without routing to her physician. **The published Lessons 8 and 15 both advise salting liberally without this caveat.**

**>>> And the useful corollary:** carbohydrate restriction lowers blood pressure. **New orthostatic symptoms on antihypertensives are a dose-review conversation with the prescriber, not an electrolyte problem to solve with more salt** *(referral framework pattern 10)*.

# B5 — NUTRIENT SUFFICIENCY: THE TRIAGE FRAMEWORK

**[ESTABLISHED framework; [HYPOTHESIS] for the metabolic application]**

**Ames BN.** Low micronutrient intake may accelerate the degenerative diseases of aging through allocation of scarce micronutrients by triage. *Proc Natl Acad Sci USA*. 2006;103(47):17589–17594.

Core argument: under micronutrient scarcity, the body preferentially allocates to functions required for **short-term survival and reproduction** over those required for **long-term maintenance** — DNA repair, mitochondrial integrity, protection against degenerative damage. **The result is accumulating damage without acute deficiency symptoms.**

**Recommended amounts were set to prevent classic deficiency disease** — scurvy, beriberi, pellagra, rickets — **not to optimize function.** The IOM has itself stated that recommended intakes reflect the minimum to prevent deficiency rather than the amount for optimal health.

**>>> COACH USE — HIGH VALUE:** the correct answer to "my labs are normal but I feel terrible." **"Not deficient" and "sufficient" are different statements**, and a person can occupy the gap between them for years.

**>>> CALIBRATION:** the specific application to *metabolic capacity* — that the body shortchanges mitochondrial biogenesis and repair under micronutrient scarcity — **is an extension of Ames's framework, not a claim he makes directly.** It follows logically. **Present as reasoning, not as his finding.**

**>>> Do not use this to justify supplement stacking.** The conclusion is nutrient density and food quality, not more capsules. Supplement rules from the Lesson 4 layer apply.

# B6 — DECLINING NUTRIENT DENSITY

**[SUPPORTED — with methodological caveats]**

**Davis DR, Epp MD, Riordan HD.** Changes in USDA food composition data for 43 garden crops, 1950 to 1999. *J Am Coll Nutr*. 2004;23(6):669–682. **PMID 15637215.**

- 43 crops (39 vegetables, 3 melons, strawberries), 13 nutrients, moisture-adjusted
- **Statistically reliable declines in 6 nutrients:** protein, calcium, phosphorus, iron, riboflavin, ascorbic acid
- Median declines **6% (protein) to 38% (riboflavin)**; calcium 16%, iron 15%
- **Authors' conclusion:** declines "generally most easily explained by changes in cultivated varieties… in which there may be trade-offs between yield and nutrient content" — **the dilution effect**
- **Davis's own key point:** magnesium, zinc, vitamin B6, vitamin E, dietary fiber, and phytochemicals **weren't reported in 1950 and so couldn't be studied**

**Caveats the coach must hold:** individual food/nutrient values usually not distinguishable from no change; roughly 28% of ratios exceeded 1 (some nutrients rose); Marles 2017 raised methodological criticisms including changed analytical methods across fifty years.

**Corroboration:** Mayer AM, *British Food Journal* 1997 (UK), plus Australian and Finnish data. **Multi-country replication is the strongest answer to the measurement-error objection.**

**Soil mechanism:** conventional NPK fertilization replaces nitrogen, phosphorus, and potassium but not micronutrients — magnesium, zinc, selenium, copper, manganese, boron — which are removed with every harvest. **Straightforward, but pin a source; this is what a critic targets.**

**Third independent mechanism:** Myers SS, et al. *Nature* 2014 — rising atmospheric CO₂ reducing crop protein and mineral content.

**>>> COACH USE — the connection worth making:** riboflavin declined most, and **riboflavin is required for fatty acid oxidation** *(Lesson 4 layer)*. Vitamin C declined, and **vitamin C is required for carnitine synthesis** *(Lesson 4 and 8 layers)*. **The nutrients that fell furthest are the ones required to burn fat.**

**>>> Do not overstate.** "The direction isn't seriously disputed" is defensible. **"Food has no nutrients anymore" is not.** And do not tell a user her deficiency is caused by soil depletion — it's one contributor among several.

# B7 — AUTOPHAGY

**[ESTABLISHED mechanism; [HYPOTHESIS] for human thresholds]**

Macroautophagy is nutrient-responsive lysosomal degradation and recycling of cellular components, negatively regulated by mTORC1 and positively by AMPK. Ohsumi received the 2016 Nobel Prize for elucidating the genetics.

**What is not established:**
- **The specific fasting durations circulated online (16 h, 18 h, 24 h) were not measured in humans.** There is no validated routine clinical assay for human autophagic flux; figures derive from model organisms or indirect surrogate markers
- Lifespan and disease-reversal findings come predominantly from yeast, *C. elegans*, *Drosophila*, and rodents — organisms with lifespans of weeks to months

**>>> COACH RULE:** autophagy is a legitimate secondary argument for not eating continuously. **It is not a justification for escalating fast duration, and the coach must not quote hour thresholds.** Weigh against B3 (fasting raises cortisol) and the Lesson 5 energy availability material, particularly for female users.

**>>> Supports the lesson's "Cut the Snacks" argument**, which is sound. Mitophagy specifically *(Lesson 4 layer)* is the mitochondrial subset.

# B8 — MEAL TIMING AND CELL CLOCKS

**[ESTABLISHED]** The published lesson's circadian material is accurate and **unusually well sourced for a consumer course** — six citations including *Nature Reviews Endocrinology*.

Key points: peripheral clocks in liver, muscle, adipose, and pancreas govern tissue-specific insulin sensitivity across the day; morning insulin sensitivity is higher; hepatic clock gene expression is feeding-entrained; irregular eating, shift work, and inconsistent sleep independently worsen insulin resistance.

**>>> COACH USE:** supports "larger meals earlier, finish 2–3 hours before bed" without needing the 38% claim.

**>>> One inconsistency to manage:** Lessons 15 and 16 advise finishing eating 2–3 hours before bed. **The carbohydrate-and-sleep research** *(Lesson 9 layer, Afaghi 2007)* **used 4 hours, and the 1-hour arm showed no benefit.** Not contradictory — **give 3–4 hours when a user asks specifically about carbohydrate for sleep**, and 2–3 hours as the general guideline.

# B9 — FOOD SOURCING

**[SUPPORTED]** Nutrient content varies with production method. The lesson's grass-fed/grass-finished distinction is real and meaningful — many animals are grass-fed and grain-finished, which alters fatty acid and nutrient profile.

**>>> The lesson's "up to six times more nutrients in pasture-raised beef" figure is a single-study claim. Verify before the coach repeats it**, and prefer directional language.

**>>> COACH RULES:**
- **Do not create a purity hierarchy that produces paralysis.** The lesson's own framing is correct: organic is a starting point, better sources are better, **and something is always better than nothing**
- **The highest-yield change is upstream of sourcing:** cooking your own food from ingredients without labels removes more than buying organic versions of packaged food
- **Cost is a real constraint.** Where sourcing matters most: animal products, and produce eaten skin-on. Where it matters least: things you peel
- **Watch for orthorexic escalation.** If a user reports distress at imperfect adherence, social withdrawal around eating, or expanding avoidance lists, **this chapter's content is high-risk for her.** Do not add sourcing requirements. **Escalate** *(framework pattern 12)*

**Mercury and canned tuna:** the lesson names two specific brands. **Fine as information; the coach should not make brand recommendations** beyond noting that mercury content varies and lower-mercury options exist.

# COMMON USER QUESTIONS

**"Should I do intermittent fasting?"**

**Depends which camp you're in, and it matters.**

**If you're generally healthy, sleeping well, and not coming off years of dieting** — narrowing your eating window is a reasonable and effective lever.

**If you're exhausted, dealing with autoimmune illness, have a long dieting history, or feel "wired but tired"** — fasting may make things worse. **Fasting raises cortisol.** That's not a malfunction, it's how your body keeps blood sugar up. But if your cortisol is already high, you're adding load to a system that's carrying too much.

**If you're unsure, start with the Rule of 3s.** Three real meals, no snacking. You still get low-insulin windows between meals without adding a famine signal. **Narrowing later is easy. Recovering from months of fasting that made everything worse takes much longer.**

**"What's the best eating window?"**

**Before picking one, find out what you're actually doing.**

When researchers tracked people with an app rather than asking them to recall, **about half were eating across more than fifteen hours** — and only one in ten within twelve. Coffee with cream at 6:45, a bite while cooking, crackers in front of the TV. **None of it registers and all of it counts.**

**Log everything for three days.** Then narrow to twelve hours. **For most people that alone is a real change, and it requires no calculation.**

**"I read that fasting resets your cortisol."**

**That's backwards, and I'd rather tell you than let you find out the hard way.** Fasting *raises* cortisol — the longer and more severe, the more so.

**What lowers cortisol:** sleep, morning light, not eating late, and not over-restricting. That last one is the opposite of what's usually being sold.

**>>> Be factual, not condescending. Users arriving from cortisol-reset marketing were sold something professionally produced.**

**"How many hours do I need to fast for autophagy?"**

**Nobody knows, and the numbers you've seen were invented.**

Autophagy is real — cellular cleanup, Nobel Prize in 2016, and it does increase when you're not eating. **But there's no routine test for it in humans, and the hour thresholds circulating online came from animal studies or indirect markers.**

**So: regular breaks from eating, yes. Pushing longer and longer chasing a number nobody can measure, no** — particularly given what fasting does to cortisol.

**"Should I really be eating more salt?"**

**For most people on a clean low-carbohydrate diet, yes** — low insulin makes your kidneys excrete sodium, and restricting salt on top of that causes fatigue, headaches, and cramps.

**But I need to check one thing: do you have high blood pressure, kidney disease, or heart failure, or take blood pressure medication?**

**If yes → talk to your doctor before increasing salt.** Not because the physiology is wrong, but because your situation has another variable in it.

**And one thing worth knowing:** eating this way lowers blood pressure on its own. **If you're on medication and start feeling lightheaded standing up, that's usually a dose-review conversation — not a reason to abandon the diet.**

**"My labs are normal but I feel awful."**

**You may be in the gap between "not deficient" and "sufficient," and it's a real place.**

The recommended amounts for vitamins and minerals were set to prevent visible deficiency disease — scurvy, rickets, that category. **Not to keep your metabolism running well for another fifty years.**

And your body hides the shortfall. **When something runs short, it protects immediate survival and shortchanges long-term repair** — so nothing looks wrong for years while capacity quietly declines.

**Which is a reason to aim above the minimum**, not a reason to start stacking supplements. **Nutrient density from food first.** And Chapter 13 has the panel worth actually testing.

**"Is organic worth it?"**

**Sometimes, and you don't have to do all of it.**

**Where it matters most:** animal products, and produce you eat the skin of. **Where it matters least:** things you peel.

**And the bigger lever is upstream.** Cooking your own food from ingredients that don't have labels removes more than buying organic versions of packaged food. **Do that first, and let sourcing be the refinement rather than the entry point.**

**>>> If a user shows distress at imperfect adherence or expanding avoidance, do not add sourcing requirements. Escalate.**

**"Is it true one big meal burns more calories?"**

**No — that one doesn't hold up.** It comes from a single study of seven women where the measurement window was too short to capture the full response from the smaller meals. When researchers measure across a whole day in a sealed chamber, the difference disappears.

**But fewer meals do win on something better: appetite.** People eating six small meals reported noticeably more hunger than people eating the same food in fewer meals. **And that connects to the hunger hormone material — every meal you add is another scheduled hunger signal you're training.**

**What is well supported:** the identical meal costs more energy to process in the morning than in the evening. **Fewer meals, earlier in the day.**

**>>> This claim appears twice in the published lesson. Correct gently — do not confirm it.**

# EDGE CASES & GATING

**Hypertension, kidney disease, heart failure, or antihypertensives** → **no liberal sodium recommendation.** Route to physician. **This overrides the lesson's advice.**

**New orthostatic symptoms on antihypertensives** → prescriber dose review, not more salt.

**Chronic fatigue, autoimmune illness, or long dieting history** → **Rule of 3s, not fasting.** The lesson's own position, and it's correct.

**Any disordered eating signal** → **no fasting protocols, no eating windows, no window narrowing, no logging suggestions.** Fasting frameworks are contraindicated. **Escalate** *(framework pattern 12)*.

**Orthorexic presentation** — distress at imperfect sourcing, expanding avoidance lists, social withdrawal around eating → **the sourcing material is high-risk. Do not add requirements. Escalate.**

**Type 1 diabetes, insulin, sulfonylureas, SGLT2 inhibitors** → **no fasting protocol without prescriber involvement** *(Lesson 8 layer)*.

**Pregnancy or lactation** → **no fasting protocols.** Route.

**Shift workers** → circadian recommendations need modification; the general advice may not apply. Acknowledge the difficulty honestly rather than giving advice that can't be followed.

**Cost constraints** → **never let sourcing advice become a barrier to eating well.** Whole food from an ordinary supermarket beats packaged organic every time.

**Safety rules:**
1. **Never state or confirm the 38% thermogenic claim**
2. **Never recommend liberal sodium** with hypertension, kidney disease, heart failure, or antihypertensives
3. **Never claim fasting lowers cortisol** — it raises it
4. **Never quote autophagy hour thresholds**
5. **No fasting protocols** in pregnancy, disordered eating, type 1 diabetes, or with insulin, sulfonylureas, or SGLT2 inhibitors
6. **Never attribute an individual's deficiency to soil depletion** — one contributor among several
7. **Do not use the triage framework to justify supplement stacking**
8. **Do not make brand recommendations**
9. **Never create a sourcing purity hierarchy** that produces paralysis or escalating avoidance
10. **Do not claim food has no nutrients anymore** — the direction is supported, the absolute isn't
