Step one

Reset & De-stress the Nervous System

Start with the nervous system, because nothing else heals well while the body is stuck in survival mode.

Step 1

Stuck in fight, flight or freeze

Short-term stress

  • Floods the blood with cortisol, insulin and glucose
  • Muscles on high alert with fuel to fight or flee
  • Powers down digestion and immunity
  • Exactly what we need to survive the moment

Chronic stress

  • Weak immunity, high blood sugar, poor digestion
  • Malnourishment and bad sleep
  • Insulin and cortisol resistance: hungry and tired at once
  • The body forgets how to turn it off

Freeze

  • When fighting or fleeing feels impossible, the body shuts down
  • Numbness, heaviness, feeling stuck or checked out
  • Low energy, low motivation, brain fog
  • Often mistaken for laziness or depression

Our bodies automatically respond to our environment. Historically, stress meant physical danger, and the body prepared to fight or flee. Continuous worry keeps us chronically in a sympathetic state, with the other important body systems downregulated or turned off.

Freeze is the third survival response. When the threat feels too big to fight or escape, the nervous system hits the brakes instead: heart rate and energy drop, and we go numb, shut down or disconnect. In the moment it protects us. When it becomes a pattern, people describe feeling stuck, exhausted, foggy, unmotivated or "not really here," and it is often labeled laziness or depression when it is actually a nervous system in protection mode.

Through various methods, we can switch back into a relaxed state and move between these responses and rest naturally and appropriately.

Step 1 · Methods

How we reset

Vagal toning

Stimulating the vagus nerve with humming, singing, gargling, cold water on the face or long exhales to switch on rest-and-digest.

Meditation

Training attention to calm the stress response and observe thoughts instead of reacting to them.

Breath work

Slow, deep breathing with longer exhales tells the nervous system it is safe.

Reducing body inflammation

Inflammation is a physical stressor. Anti-inflammatory food and habits lower the load.

Adrenal reset

Supporting a healthy cortisol rhythm after chronic stress with balanced blood sugar, rest and nutrients.

Circadian balancing & sleep

Morning light, consistent sleep and wake times, and darkness at night reset the body clock.

Trauma work

Releasing stored emotional experiences that keep the body on high alert.

Light exercise

Walking and yoga burn off stress hormones without adding more stress.

Step 2 · Sugar

Sugar, insulin & the brain

  • Sugar raises insulin, the fat-storing hormone. Insulin moves sugar out of the blood and into storage, mostly as fat, and while it's high the body can't burn stored fat.
  • Insulin drives appetite and inflammation. It raises hunger hormones, cholesterol and blood pressure, so we crave more and the body stays inflamed.
  • Chronically high insulin affects the whole body. It makes blood more likely to clot, increases oxidative stress, lowers B vitamins and throws off sex hormones, which can affect fertility.
  • Sugar uses up vitamins and minerals. Processing it takes B vitamins, magnesium and chromium, but sugar brings none of its own.
  • Sugar forms AGEs. Sugar binds to proteins and creates advanced glycation end products, a sticky crust that stiffens tissues and gums up the brain.
  • Sugar steals from serotonin. It pushes tryptophan, the building block of serotonin, down a different pathway, leaving less for mood and sleep.

Inflammation from high insulin is linked to depression, panic attacks, anxiety, insomnia and ADHD. Those with insulin resistance have a threefold risk of severe depression.

AGEs, advanced glycation end products, are like the crust on a crème brûlée.

High sugar increases the enzyme that breaks down tryptophan, the amino acid that makes serotonin, pushing it down a path that makes glutamate instead.

Step 2

Gluten

  • Gluten raises zonulin. Zonulin opens the tight junctions between gut cells, loosening the barrier that keeps food and bacteria out of the blood.
  • Leaky gut lets particles through. Undigested proteins, including opium-like gluteomorphins, reach the bloodstream and can alter brain function and behavior.
  • The brain barrier can loosen too. Zonulin also affects the blood-brain barrier, letting inflammation reach the brain.
  • It can trigger autoimmunity. Some gluten proteins resemble our own tissues, like the thyroid, so the immune system may attack both.

Celiac disease

An autoimmune disease. Gluten makes the immune system attack the small intestine and flatten the villi that absorb nutrients. Genetic, diagnosed with blood antibodies and biopsy, and requires strict, lifelong avoidance, even of crumbs.

Gluten sensitivity

Real reactions without the autoimmune damage to the intestine. There is no single test; it's found by ruling out celiac and then removing gluten. Symptoms can still be significant, and tolerance varies by person.

Common signs: digestive issues · fatigue · iron-deficiency anemia · weight loss · joint pain · itchy blistering rash · mouth ulcers · headaches · numbness or tingling · brain fog · depression or anxiety · infertility · delayed growth in kids

Many people with celiac disease are undiagnosed, and many more have gluten intolerance.

Celiac vs. sensitivity: with celiac, the immune system damages the intestine itself, and even a crumb matters. With sensitivity, the reaction is real but the intestine isn't damaged the same way. A third, separate condition is a wheat allergy, a classic allergic reaction like hives or swelling.

The undigested particles include gluteomorphins, opium-like proteins.

Gluten is associated with anxiety, depression, schizophrenia, dementia, headaches, epilepsy, autoimmune and thyroid disease, autism and more.

Step 2

Seed oils

Chemical processing

  • Hexane: a petroleum solvent used to pull oil out of seeds; it is neurotoxic in high exposure
  • Sodium hydroxide (lye): a caustic chemical used to strip out free fatty acids
  • Phosphoric acid: a corrosive used to "degum" the oil before it's bleached and deodorized

High heat

  • Alters the structure: these fragile fats aren't built for high heat in processing or frying
  • Oxidized and rancid: the oil goes bad before it reaches you, then deodorizing hides the smell
  • Forms aldehydes: toxic compounds that inflame and damage cells, including brain cells, and are linked to dementia

Omega-6 overload

  • Oxidative stress: excess omega-6 fats oxidize easily inside the body
  • Chronic inflammation: they crowd out anti-inflammatory omega-3s in our cells
  • Slow to clear: they're stored in body fat and cell membranes and can take months to years to replace

Aldehydes cause inflammation and contribute to Alzheimer's disease and other forms of dementia. Seed oils can take 6 months to 5 years to fully process and remove from the body.

Step 2 · Dairy

Why is dairy so hard on us?

Antibiotics

Cows are given antibiotics preventatively. This affects their health and microbiome and contributes to antibiotic resistance in us.

Hormones

Estrogen, progesterone, growth hormone and oxytocin are used to raise milk output and can affect our own hormones.

Diet & living conditions

Crowded barns and a diet of corn and feed instead of grass change the nutritional value of the milk.

Cooked to death

Pasteurizing and homogenizing kill the good bacteria and enzymes that help us break down milk proteins.

A1 casein

Modern breeds make A1 casein, which breaks down into BCM-7, a peptide linked to gut inflammation. Original A2 milk is easier to digest.

Lactose

Milk sugar needs the enzyme lactase, and most of us make less and less of it as we age, leading to gas, bloating and diarrhea.

Cows live in tight conditions with excess ammonia and are fed corn and other feed that isn't their natural diet. Breeding for higher production changed the casein protein from A2 to A1.

Step 2 · What to eat

Foods that love us back

Eat a wide variety of bright, colorful vegetables and fruits.

  • Nourish the whole body. Vegetables and fruits supply the vitamins, minerals and enzymes every cell needs for optimal function and a healthy metabolism.
  • Feed your good gut bacteria. Their fiber feeds the microbes that help make serotonin and dopamine and train the immune system.
  • The richer the color, the better. Deep reds, purples, oranges and greens signal more phytonutrients and antioxidants, and each color brings different ones.
  • Calm inflammation and lift mood. Antioxidants clear oxidative stress and reduce inflammation, which helps with depression, ADHD and more.

Encourage everyone to "eat the rainbow" every day.

Step 2 · What to eat

Meat

  • Choose grass-fed beef and organic poultry. How an animal is raised changes its meat: grass-fed beef has more omega-3s and antioxidants and fewer added hormones and antibiotics.
  • Packed with nutrients. Properly raised meat is rich in healthy fats, vitamins A and E, B6, B12, folate and zinc, in forms the body uses easily.
  • Protein builds your brain chemistry. Every neurotransmitter and hormone, including serotonin and dopamine, is made from amino acids, the building blocks of protein.
  • Easier to absorb. Animal protein is more complete and more easily broken down and absorbed than most plant proteins.

Key nutrients from meat

  • B12: nerves, energy and red blood cells; not found in plants
  • Creatine: energy for muscles and the brain
  • Carnosine: an antioxidant that protects muscle and brain tissue
  • DHA omega-3: the main structural fat of the brain

Organ meats are the most nutrient-dense foods in the world.

Liver is especially rich in vitamin A, B12, folate, iron, copper and choline.

All of our neurotransmitters and other hormones are derived from amino acids, in other words, protein.

How beliefs get wired

  • Trauma builds strong pathways. In a heightened physical and emotional state, the brain wires deep connections so it can prevent or prepare for the same thing happening again.
  • Children learn in delta waves. Until about age 7 or 8, the brain stays in a slow wave state that absorbs everything, including ideas about who we are and how we fit in.
  • Early beliefs become our wiring. Negative childhood experiences turn into rooted subconscious beliefs we carry into adulthood without realizing it.
  • Affirmations alone aren't enough. These beliefs live in the nervous system, so they have to be released and rewired, not just talked over.

Beliefs like

"I'm a bad kid."
"I need to stay out of the way."
"I will never be enough."

These memories become part of our emotional response and neurological wiring. Rewiring takes going back into those brain wave states and releasing and rewiring the rooted emotions and experiences.

Step 5

Common gut problems

Leaky gut

Under a thin mucus layer, a single layer of cells separates us from everything we eat. When it's damaged, bacteria, food particles and chemicals reach the blood, driving inflammation and autoimmunity.

SIBO / SIFO

The small intestine should be nearly sterile. Poor digestion or low stomach acid lets bacteria or fungus overgrow there, causing gas and bloating.

Low acid, bile & enzymes

Modern diets, overeating and toxic overload leave us short on what we need to break food down.

Dysbiosis

An imbalance of bacteria, fungus or parasites, fueled by over-sterilization, medications and poor food quality.

Gastroparesis

The stomach stops its normal contractions and food sits too long, often from stress or parasites.

These often overlap. Low stomach acid can lead to SIBO, which can damage the lining and lead to leaky gut.

Step 5

Symptoms of gut issues

Gut problems rarely stay in the gut. Inflammation, missing nutrients and the gut-brain connection spread symptoms through the whole body.

Digestive

  • Bloating, gas
  • Food sensitivities (gluten, dairy, soy)
  • Diarrhea or loose stools
  • Constipation
  • Heartburn, reflux
  • IBS-like symptoms

Inflammatory & immune

  • Chronic fatigue
  • Brain fog
  • Headaches, migraines
  • Joint pain or stiffness
  • Eczema, acne, psoriasis, rosacea
  • Allergies, asthma
  • Histamine intolerance

Mental & emotional

  • Anxiety, depression
  • Irritability, mood swings
  • Poor memory or attention
  • Sleep disturbances
  • ADHD-like symptoms

Whole body

  • Low B12, iron, zinc, magnesium
  • Frequent infections
  • Unexplained weight gain or loss
  • Sugar and carb cravings from microbial imbalance

Ask: how many of these do you have? Most people are surprised how many trace back to the gut.

Step 6

What builds up in us

Food additivesPesticidesMicroplasticsArsenicMercuryCadmiumLeadAluminumFluoridePFAS / forever chemicals

Heavy metals and forever chemicals settle into fat, bone and organs, disrupting cell function and hormone signals.

  • Many never leave on their own. The body stores what it can't clear, and the load builds year after year.
  • Genes matter. Variants in MTHFR, NFE2L2 and cytochrome P450 can slow the body's ability to detox, and research links them to issues like cancer, depression and Parkinson's.
  • Stress makes it worse. Chronic stress downregulates detoxification.
  • Two jobs: avoid new toxins, and help the body release what's already stored.

Not only do we need to learn how to avoid these toxins but also how to support our bodies to remove what is trapped inside of our tissues.

Step 6 · Methods

What can I do about toxins?

Remove major sources

  • Eat organic, starting with the Dirty Dozen and grains
  • Remove processed foods
  • Filter your water; even city and spring water can carry metals
  • Safe cookware; never heat food in plastic
  • Glass food storage
  • Check for mold
  • Skip artificial scents and candles
  • Clean beauty and cleaning products (EWG.org)
  • Air filters and plants

Lower the body's load

  • Exercise and reduce stress to switch detox back on
  • Support the liver, the main detox organ
  • Saunas to sweat toxins out
  • Cruciferous veggies, cilantro, spirulina
  • Minerals, electrolytes and hydration
  • Mitochondrial and glutathione support
  • Gut health, bile and binders to carry toxins out
  • Sun exposure and grounding
  • Lymphatic drainage: yoga, dry brushing, massage

Start with the easy swaps at home, then support the body's own detox systems. Chelation therapy is an option for heavy metals under a practitioner's care.
